### GENDERED NOUN FORMATTING

When a noun can naturally refer to either a male or female person AND Spanish uses different masculine/feminine forms, represent BOTH forms in the `spanish` field.

The formatting depends on how the feminine form is formed.

#### Masculine -o / feminine -a

When the masculine form ends in `-o` and the feminine form is formed by changing the final `-o` to `-a`, use:

"masculine/a"

Examples:

"niño" → "niño/a"
"maestro" → "maestro/a"
"amigo" → "amigo/a"
"empleado" → "empleado/a"
"cajero" → "cajero/a"
"jugador" → NOT "jugador/a" because it does not end in -o

IMPORTANT:
Do NOT use the `(a)` format for words ending in `-o` whose feminine form ends in `-a`.

Correct:
"cajero/a"
"maestro/a"
"trabajador(a)"

Incorrect:
"cajero(a)"
"maestro(a)"

#### Masculine forms where feminine adds -a

When the masculine form ends in a consonant and the feminine form is created by adding `-a`, use:

"masculine(a)"

Examples:

"trabajador" → "trabajador(a)"
"profesor" → "profesor(a)"
"vendedor" → "vendedor(a)"
"autor" → "autor(a)"
"director" → "director(a)"

The `(a)` represents the additional feminine `a`.

#### Irregular or substantially different feminine forms

When the masculine and feminine forms are genuinely distinct and cannot appropriately be represented using either `-o/a` or `(a)`, use the actual masculine and feminine forms separated by `/` with NO spaces.

Format:

"masculine/feminine"

Examples:

"actor/actriz"
"rey/reina"
"héroe/heroína"

NEVER invent forms such as:

"actor(a)"
"rey(a)"
"héroe(a)"
"actor/actora"
"rey/reya"

Only use an irregular feminine form when you are highly confident that it is the correct Spanish form.

#### Gender-specific nouns

If a noun refers specifically to males or specifically to females, do NOT create a combined form.

Examples:

"hombre"
"padre"
"esposo"

remain masculine.

Examples:

"mujer"
"madre"
"esposa"

remain feminine.

Do NOT convert these to `common`.

#### Important distinction: common vs. epicene nouns

A noun is `common` when its form changes according to the sex of the person being described.

Examples:

"niño/niña"
"trabajador/trabajadora"
"profesor/profesora"

A noun is `epicene` when it has ONE fixed grammatical gender but can refer to a person of either sex without changing the noun.

Examples:

"persona" → feminine epicene
"víctima" → feminine epicene
"testigo" → masculine epicene
"miembro" → masculine epicene

Do NOT classify a noun as `common` merely because both men and women can be described using it.

Do NOT classify a noun as `epicene` merely because its masculine and feminine forms happen to look identical.

Determine the actual grammatical behavior of the Spanish noun.

#### Irregular gender pairs

When masculine and feminine forms are separate lexical forms rather than a simple gender variation, DO NOT combine them into one vocabulary entry if the meanings are distinct.

For example:

"rey" = King
"reina" = Queen

These should remain separate entries rather than:

"rey/reina" = "King/Queen"

Likewise:

"actor" = Actor
"actriz" = Actress

These should remain separate entries when they are provided or generated as distinct vocabulary items.

The combined slash format is intended for a SINGLE vocabulary concept represented by both gender forms, not for combining unrelated lexical entries.

### ADJECTIVE GENDER FORMATTING

For adjectives, the same gender-representation principle applies, but ONLY the `spanish` field is affected.

Do NOT modify `gender_type` or `gender` merely because an adjective has masculine/feminine forms.

#### Adjectives ending in -o

When an adjective has masculine `-o` and feminine `-a`, use:

"masculine/a"

Examples:

"alto" → "alto/a"
"bajo" → "bajo/a"
"bonito" → "bonito/a"
"cansado" → "cansado/a"
"loco" → "loco/a"
"trabajador" does NOT belong to this category because it does not end in `-o`.

#### Adjectives where feminine adds -a

When the masculine adjective ends in a consonant and the feminine form is created by adding `-a`, use:

"masculine(a)"

Examples:

"trabajador" → "trabajador(a)"
"español" → "español(a)"

Only use `(a)` when the feminine form is created by adding `a`.

#### Adjectives with distinct masculine/feminine forms

When the masculine and feminine adjective forms are genuinely distinct and cannot be represented with `-o/a` or `(a)`, separate them with `/` and NO spaces.

Format:

"masculine/feminine"

Use the actual feminine form.

Do NOT invent feminine forms.

#### Invariable adjectives

If the adjective has the same form for masculine and feminine, do NOT modify it.

Examples:

"grande"
"feliz"
"amable"
"interesante"
"inteligente"

Do NOT add `/a` or `(a)` merely because the adjective can describe both males and females.

#### Do not apply suffix rules blindly

Do NOT determine gender formatting solely from spelling.

For every adjective, determine whether the feminine form actually changes.

For example:

"rojo" → "rojo/a"
"joven" → "joven"
"feliz" → "feliz"

Do not modify an adjective if its masculine and feminine forms are identical.

Values:

### regular

Use `regular` for a noun with a fixed grammatical gender that is NOT an epicene noun.

Examples:

"hombre" → regular / M
"mujer" → regular / F
"rey" → regular / M
"reina" → regular / F

### common

Use `common` when the noun has masculine and feminine forms and can naturally refer to either sex.

Examples:

"niño/a" → common / U
"trabajador(a)" → common / U
"profesor(a)" → common / U

The `spanish` field MUST represent both forms for common nouns.

### epicene

Use `epicene` when:

1. the noun has ONE fixed grammatical gender,
2. the noun can refer to either a male or female person,
3. the noun itself does NOT change according to the person's sex.

Examples:

"persona" → epicene / F
"víctima" → epicene / F
"testigo" → epicene / M
"miembro" → epicene / M

IMPORTANT:

`epicene` does NOT mean "gender-neutral."

An epicene noun still has grammatical gender.

Therefore:

"persona" → gender = F
"testigo" → gender = M

Never use `U` for epicene nouns.

### CRITICAL COMMON VS. EPICENE DECISION

Ask these questions in order:

1. Can this noun refer to either a male or female person?

   * If NO: use `regular` with M or F.
   * If YES: continue.

2. Does the noun change form depending on the person's sex?

   * If YES: use `common` and `gender = U`.
   * If NO: continue.

3. Does the noun retain one fixed grammatical gender regardless of the person's sex?

   * If YES: use `epicene` and set M or F according to grammatical gender.

Do NOT use `common` merely because both men and women can be described by the noun.

Do NOT use `epicene` merely because the noun has the same spelling for both sexes.
